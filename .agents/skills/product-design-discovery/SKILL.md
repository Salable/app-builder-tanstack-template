# Product design discovery

Turn the accepted product direction into design decisions an author can implement.
Reuse supplied answers, brand assets and repository context. Preserve the chosen
stack, scope and commercial terms; recommendations remain optional until accepted.

## Guide the conversation

Ask helpful, contextual questions about the audience, desired impression, content
and visual preferences. Recommend plausible choices with short reasons instead of
asking the user to design the site from scratch. Ask at most two material questions
per turn, following the current planning conversation. Do not repeat answered
questions or turn discovery into a compulsory questionnaire.

Treat responsive behavior, accessibility, safe handling of data, appropriate SEO
and performance as automatic engineering baselines. Do not ask whether the user
wants them or make them choose routine implementation details. Ask only about a
material product preference or constraint that cannot be inferred.

For public monetised products, default to public marketing/pricing and signup
before purchase unless accepted requirements specify another journey. Public
pricing does not require anonymous checkout. Do not add billing to a free product,
invent prices or select a billing model through a visual-design recommendation.

Read [guided discovery and research](references/discovery-and-research.md) when
eliciting missing direction or investigating the market. Read
[visual directions](references/visual-directions.md) when translating preferences
into composition or when useful alternatives would help the user choose.

## Preserve planning authority

Record audience, primary visitor job, page journey, visual direction, content
constraints and unresolved assumptions in the existing permitted brief/spec
content. Tie a substantial presentation change to an owning ticket and local
acceptance criteria when generating those artifacts. Never silently add fields to
a strict schema or create a new approval stage.

Dialogue alone does not generate or revise artifacts, approve work or advance
planning. Present research findings and optional alternatives within the current
stage; use the platform's explicit commands for changes and continuation.

When the user delegates design, recommend a suitable direction, identify the
assumptions briefly and continue through the existing flow. Two or three distinct
directions can help an undecided user, but are not a required deliverable or gate.
Do not claim stakeholder preference, competitor imitation or an agent review is
evidence of customer usability.
